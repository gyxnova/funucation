# Visual treatment

## The idea

Simple geometric shapes act out a concept while a song explains it. The viewer should be able to follow the idea even when the music is muted: signals move, structures respond, and cause and effect stay visible. Every frame is a pure function of song time, so a moment can be replayed, scrubbed, or rendered as a still without changing its meaning.

## Tone

Dynamic and on the beat, with dry humor in the timing and behavior rather than in decorative gags. Keep the image direct, legible, and composed; no clip-art, purple/cyan neon, or emoji.

## Palette

| Name | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#0A0A0A` | Main background |
| Ink 2 | `#151517` | Panels and quiet surfaces |
| Graphite | `#5E5B57` | Secondary structure and labels |
| Bone | `#EEE9DF` | Type, diagrams, and unlit information |
| Signal | `#FF4D12` | Active data and attention |
| Ember | `#FF8A3D` | Secondary data and warm highlights |
| Dim | `#3A3A3E` | Inactive marks and separators |

## Typography

Use a system rounded bold sans stack for lyrics: large, clear, and short enough to read at a glance. Use monospace for numbers, variables, and labels. Keep generous negative space around both type and moving shapes. Do not load external fonts.

Scene typography has hard minimums: labels are at least **28px**; numbers and equations are at least **40px**. Do not shrink text below these sizes to fit a composition; simplify or reposition it instead.

## Karaoke rules

Each lyric line is readable and highlighted in sync with its own start and end. The sung portion uses bone and signal; the unsung portion remains visible at 35% opacity. Keep lyric text inside a 96px safe area on every edge. Lyric windows are **TBD** for all plates until timed to the final song.

## Motifs

The **SPARK** is an orange point with a white-hot core dragging a hairline: it is the signal flowing through the mechanism. Plastic orange shapes are only for data. Use hairline bone diagrams for structure. The spark is the only glow.
